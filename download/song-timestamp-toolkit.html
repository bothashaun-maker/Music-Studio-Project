Stamp Every Song
Three kinds of timestamps power every great music video: the song’s structure, the lyrics, and the beat. This guide gives you the best free route for each one — researched across GitHub, the web and YouTube in November 2026, with every link verified live and every pick chosen for a Windows PC and a zero-code workflow.

Part 01
The Short Answer

“Time-stamping a song” actually means three different jobs, and each one feeds your music video in a different way. A structure map tells you where the intro, verses, choruses and outro land, so you can plan which footage hits at which moment. Synced lyrics put the right words on screen at the right second, as an LRC or SRT file. A beat grid gives your cuts a pulse, so every clip change lands on the music instead of drifting past it. The three research passes behind this guide tested the popular options honestly — several famous tools turned out to be dead, broken or paid — and what survived is below.

I want a song structure map Moises
Free tierDifficulty 1 / 5About 5 min

Upload your WAV or MP3 at moises.ai and the AI marks Intro, Verse, Chorus and more directly on a timeline. Fastest labeled route for your own YuE2 renders; a YouTube-URL fallback exists via SONOTELLER, and an offline free-forever route via Sonic Visualiser.

I want synced lyrics QuickLRC
FreeDifficulty 1 / 5About 10 min

You wrote the lyrics, so a tap-along editor guarantees 100 percent correct text where speech-to-text AI mishears sung vocals. QuickLRC exports SRT for editors and LRC for players from the same session.

I want beat-locked cuts CapCut
FreeDifficulty 1 / 5About 5 min

CapCut Desktop’s built-in Beat Sync detects the beats, drops markers on the timeline and can auto-align clips to them — detection, markers and cutting in one free app, with no external files to juggle.

Part 02
Song Structure Maps

A structure map is the planning layer of a music video. When you know the chorus starts at 1:07, you know the hook visual goes there, the verse footage stays conversational, and the bridge is your slow-motion or tonal-shift moment. Because YuE2 decides its own arrangement, your song’s real structure is only discoverable from the audio itself — these three routes read it for you, from easiest to most manual.

Route A — Moises Song Parts Web app
Free tierDifficulty 1 / 5File upload
1
Go to moises.ai and create a free account (web or app).
2
Upload your YuE2 song as WAV or MP3 from your PC.
3
Open the Song Parts / Sections view: an AI timeline with Intro, Verse, Chorus and more.
4
Write the times into your edit plan — pair them with the beat workflow in Part 05.

Caveat: the feature page confirms AI-detected sections on upload, but the exact free-tier limits were not stated publicly — test with your account first. If sections are gated, use Route B.

Route C — Sonic Visualiser + Segmentino Offline
Free foreverDifficulty 2.5 / 5Windows installer
1
Install Sonic Visualiser (64-bit Windows MSI) from sonicvisualiser.org/download.html.
2
Install the free Vamp Plugin Pack from vamp-plugins.org/pack.html — it bundles Segmentino.
3
Open your song, then menu Transform → Analysis by Segmentino → Segmentation.
4
Segmentino draws numbered segments (1, 2, 3…) with boundary times — listen and rename them yourself: the segment that repeats is usually your chorus.

Tutorial (verified)
Introduction to Sonic Visualiser — youtube.com/watch?v=Dc3GwR83up4

Pro option, parked: the research-grade All-In-One Music Structure Analyzer (github.com/mir-aidj/all-in-one, MIT license) outputs exactly what a video editor wants — BPM, beat and downbeat times, and segments labeled intro / verse / chorus in one JSON file, plus a PDF visualization. It is parked because the Windows install requires building the NATTEN dependency from source, and its HuggingFace demo was down (HTTP 503) at research time. Revisit it when you are comfortable with Python — everything else in this guide needs none.

Part 03
Synced Lyrics (LRC / SRT)

Here is the insight that saves you hours: you already know the exact lyrics — you wrote them. Automatic transcription (Whisper-style AI) is built for speech, and sung vocals with reverb, harmonies and stretched syllables are exactly where it garbles words, which means you would retype every line anyway. A manual tap-along editor flips the workflow: the text is perfect from the start, and you only supply the timing, one keypress per line. Ten minutes per song, and the result drops straight into your editor.

The pick — QuickLRC Tap-to-Sync Browser
FreeDifficulty 1 / 5SRT + LRC out
1
Open quicklrc.com/lrc-maker in your browser.
2
Paste your lyrics — one line per row, exactly as sung.
3
Load your song (WAV or MP3 from the YuE2 run ZIP you downloaded).
4
Press play and tap the sync key at the start of every sung line.
5
Nudge any line that feels early or late, then export SRT (for CapCut / Resolve / Premiere) and LRC (for players).
Tutorial (verified)
LRC Maker | QuickLRC — youtube.com/watch?v=N2oMYKALpTo

Getting it on screen is one import. CapCut Desktop accepts SRT, LRC and ASS files directly (tutorial: youtube.com/watch?v=PHyzxofiWo0). DaVinci Resolve (free) imports SRT into a timeline subtitle track from the Media Pool (tutorial: youtube.com/watch?v=GlofcO66pgY). Style the captions once, copy the track, and every future video inherits the look.

Upgrade path — karaoke word timing Later
OptionalWord-levelAll free

QuickLRC Enhanced generator (quicklrc.com/enhanced-lrc-generator) adds word-by-word highlighting for karaoke-style lyric videos. ESPnet Forced Alignment (huggingface.co/spaces/espnet/forced-alignment) takes your audio plus your known text and returns a start and end time for every line — the rare tool that accepts supplied lyrics; free spaces sleep, so the first visit may take a minute to wake. WhisperX (github.com/m-bain/whisperX) auto-transcribes with word timestamps via its free Colab (colab.research.google.com/github/ardha27/WhisperX-Youtube-SRT) — handy for other people’s songs, but for your own lyrics the tap-along route is faster and more accurate.

Part 04
Beat-Locked Cuts

Cutting on the beat is the difference between a video that feels edited and one that feels choreographed. The dream is beat timestamps arriving as markers in your editor, and the research found three honest ways to get there: one built into a free editor, one plugin for the pro-grade free editor, and one plain-text route that works with any tool you will ever use.

A quick word on what a marker is, because editors use the word loosely: it is a labeled flag pinned to an exact time on the timeline. Beat tools create one marker per detected beat (or per bar, which is every few beats and usually more practical for cutting). Once markers exist, snapping a clip edge to one is a single drag in every major editor — and that drag is the whole trick behind “cutting on the beat”.

Route A — CapCut Desktop Beat Sync Built-in
FreeDifficulty 1 / 5Windows app
1
Download CapCut Desktop free from capcut.com.
2
Import your song and footage; drop the song on the timeline first.
3
Select the audio and run Beat Sync (auto detection), or tap markers manually while it plays.
4
Cut your clips to the markers — or let auto-align snap clips onto beats for you.

CapCut’s own guides document both automatic and manual beat syncing; some premium effects sit behind Pro, but the beat workflow is shown free in official tutorials.

Tutorials (verified)

Route B — DaVinci Resolve free + beat plugin Markers
FreeDifficulty 3 / 5Windows only

The community plugin davinci-beat-marker-plugin (github.com/urmamge/davinci-beat-marker-plugin) detects beats and bars in any song on your Resolve timeline and places color-coded markers automatically. A one-click PowerShell installer sets up everything (Python plus the librosa detection engine) — no coding. The README confirms it works in the free version of Resolve, but note: Blackmagic tightened free-version scripting from Resolve 19.1 onward, so test on the version you run before relying on it in production.

Tutorial (verified)
FREE Automatic Beat Markers in DaVinci Resolve — youtube.com/watch?v=pcWbYHVadWE
Route C — a plain timestamp list Any editor
FreeDifficulty 2 / 5TXT output

Audacity (audacityteam.org): open the song, Analyze → Beat Finder, then File → Export → Export Labels for a tab-separated .txt of timestamps. Audacity’s manual honestly calls Beat Finder “rough and ready” on modern compressed music — treat it as onset hints, not gospel. For true beat timestamps, the command-line tool aubio ships a Windows build (aubio.org/bin/0.4.6/aubio-0.4.6-win64.zip) whose aubiotrack command prints one beat time per line — accurate, but typing commands in cmd.exe earns it a 4/5 difficulty.

Paid reference: BeatEdit for Premiere Pro is USD 99.99 and BeatEdit for DaVinci Resolve is USD 49.99 (aescripts.com) — both excellent, but the Resolve edition now requires Studio 19.1+, so the free plugin above is the sensible starting point. Skip them until editing revenue pays for them.

Part 05
YouTube Chapters + The Full Pipeline

Timestamps are not only for your editing timeline — YouTube turns them into clickable chapters on the watch page, and viewers love them on music videos. The official rules (support.google.com/youtube/answer/9884579) are simple: the first timestamp must be 00:00, you need at least three in ascending order, each chapter must be at least ten seconds long, and the list goes straight into the video description. Your structure map from Part 02 is the chapters list — write it once, use it twice.

The full JJP pipeline for one YuE2 song Six steps
1
Generate on Kaggle. Run the YuE2 ComfyUI canvas, queue the prompt, and download the complete run ZIP from the wrapper — the WAV lives inside.
2
Map the structure. Upload the WAV to Moises Song Parts (or SONOTELLER once the song is on YouTube). Write down intro / verse / chorus / outro times.
3
Tap the lyrics. QuickLRC tap-along with your written lyrics; export SRT and LRC.
4
Edit on the beat. CapCut Desktop: Beat Sync for markers, import the SRT, and land your hook visual exactly where the structure map says the chorus starts.
5
Export and publish. Render, upload to YouTube, keep the song unlisted first if you want a SONOTELLER cross-check.
6
Chapters in the description. Start at 00:00, three-plus timestamps, ten-second minimum — straight from step 2’s notes.
