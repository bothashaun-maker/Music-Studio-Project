   256→    &ldquo;Time-stamping a song&rdquo; actually means three different jobs, and each one feeds
   257→    your music video in a different way. A <b>structure map</b> tells you where the intro,
   258→    verses, choruses and outro land, so you can plan which footage hits at which moment.
   259→    <b>Synced lyrics</b> put the right words on screen at the right second, as an LRC or SRT
   260→    file. A <b>beat grid</b> gives your cuts a pulse, so every clip change lands on the
   261→    music instead of drifting past it. The three research passes behind this guide tested
   262→    the popular options honestly &mdash; several famous tools turned out to be dead, broken
   263→    or paid &mdash; and what survived is below.

   356→  <!-- ---------- 3. LYRICS ---------- -->
   357→  <div class="chapter-header">
   358→    <div class="section-tag">Part 03</div>
   359→    <div class="section-title">Synced Lyrics (LRC / SRT)</div>
   360→  </div>
   361→  <p class="body-text">
   362→    Here is the insight that saves you hours: <b>you already know the exact lyrics</b> &mdash; you
   363→    wrote them. Automatic transcription (Whisper-style AI) is built for speech, and sung vocals
   364→    with reverb, harmonies and stretched syllables are exactly where it garbles words, which
   365→    means you would retype every line anyway. A manual tap-along editor flips the workflow:
   366→    the text is perfect from the start, and you only supply the timing, one keypress per line.
   367→    Ten minutes per song, and the result drops straight into your editor.
   368→  </p>
   369→  <div class="card">
   370→    <div class="card-title">The pick &mdash; QuickLRC Tap-to-Sync <span class="pill">Browser</span></div>
   371→    <div class="fact-row">
   372→      <span class="fact">Free</span><span class="fact">Difficulty 1 / 5</span><span class="fact">SRT + LRC out</span>
   373→    </div>
   374→    <div class="step"><div class="step-num">1</div><div class="step-body">Open <a href="https://quicklrc.com/lrc-maker">quicklrc.com/lrc-maker</a> in your browser.</div></div>
   375→    <div class="step"><div class="step-num">2</div><div class="step-body">Paste your lyrics &mdash; one line per row, exactly as sung.</div></div>
   376→    <div class="step"><div class="step-num">3</div><div class="step-body">Load your song (WAV or MP3 from the YuE2 run ZIP you downloaded).</div></div>
   377→    <div class="step"><div class="step-num">4</div><div class="step-body">Press play and tap the sync key at the start of every sung line.</div></div>
   378→    <div class="step"><div class="step-num">5</div><div class="step-body">Nudge any line that feels early or late, then export <b>SRT</b> (for CapCut / Resolve / Premiere) and <b>LRC</b> (for players).</div></div>
   379→    <div class="tuts">
   380→      <div class="tuts-title">Tutorial (verified)</div>
   381→      <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N2oMYKALpTo">LRC Maker | QuickLRC &mdash; youtube.com/watch?v=N2oMYKALpTo</a>
   382→    </div>
   383→  </div>
   384→  <p class="body-text">
   385→    Getting it on screen is one import. <b>CapCut Desktop</b> accepts SRT, LRC and ASS files
   386→    directly (tutorial: youtube.com/watch?v=PHyzxofiWo0). <b>DaVinci Resolve</b> (free) imports
   387→    SRT into a timeline subtitle track from the Media Pool (tutorial: youtube.com/watch?v=GlofcO66pgY).
   388→    Style the captions once, copy the track, and every future video inherits the look.
   389→  </p>
   390→  <div class="card">
   391→    <div class="card-title">Upgrade path &mdash; karaoke word timing <span class="pill">Later</span></div>
   392→    <div class="fact-row">
   393→      <span class="fact">Optional</span><span class="fact">Word-level</span><span class="fact">All free</span>
   394→    </div>
   395→    <p><b>QuickLRC Enhanced generator</b> (quicklrc.com/enhanced-lrc-generator) adds word-by-word
   396→    highlighting for karaoke-style lyric videos. <b>ESPnet Forced Alignment</b>
   397→    (huggingface.co/spaces/espnet/forced-alignment) takes your audio <i>plus your known text</i>
   398→    and returns a start and end time for every line &mdash; the rare tool that accepts supplied
   399→    lyrics; free spaces sleep, so the first visit may take a minute to wake. <b>WhisperX</b>
   400→    (github.com/m-bain/whisperX) auto-transcribes with word timestamps via its free Colab
   401→    (colab.research.google.com/github/ardha27/WhisperX-Youtube-SRT) &mdash; handy for other
   402→    people&rsquo;s songs, but for your own lyrics the tap-along route is faster and more accurate.</p>
   403→  </div>
   404→
   405→  <!-- ---------- 4. BEATS ---------- -->
   406→  <div class="chapter-header">
   407→    <div class="section-tag">Part 04</div>
   408→    <div class="section-title">Beat-Locked Cuts</div>
   409→  </div>
   410→  <p class="body-text">
   411→    Cutting on the beat is the difference between a video that feels edited and one that
   412→    feels choreographed. The dream is beat timestamps arriving as markers in your editor,
   413→    and the research found three honest ways to get there: one built into a free editor,
   414→    one plugin for the pro-grade free editor, and one plain-text route that works with
   415→    any tool you will ever use.
   416→  </p>
   417→  <p class="body-text">
   418→    A quick word on what a marker is, because editors use the word loosely: it is a
   419→    labeled flag pinned to an exact time on the timeline. Beat tools create one marker
   420→    per detected beat (or per bar, which is every few beats and usually more practical
   421→    for cutting). Once markers exist, snapping a clip edge to one is a single drag in
   422→    every major editor &mdash; and that drag is the whole trick behind &ldquo;cutting on the beat&rdquo;.
   423→  </p>
   424→  <div class="card">
   425→    <div class="card-title">Route A &mdash; CapCut Desktop Beat Sync <span class="pill">Built-in</span></div>
   426→    <div class="fact-row">
   427→      <span class="fact">Free</span><span class="fact">Difficulty 1 / 5</span><span class="fact">Windows app</span>
   428→    </div>
   429→    <div class="step"><div class="step-num">1</div><div class="step-body">Download CapCut Desktop free from <a href="https://www.capcut.com">capcut.com</a>.</div></div>
   430→    <div class="step"><div class="step-num">2</div><div class="step-body">Import your song and footage; drop the song on the timeline first.</div></div>
   431→    <div class="step"><div class="step-num">3</div><div class="step-body">Select the audio and run <b>Beat Sync</b> (auto detection), or tap markers manually while it plays.</div></div>
   432→    <div class="step"><div class="step-num">4</div><div class="step-body">Cut your clips to the markers &mdash; or let auto-align snap clips onto beats for you.</div></div>
   433→    <p style="margin-top:10px;">CapCut&rsquo;s own guides document both automatic and manual beat syncing; some premium
   434→    effects sit behind Pro, but the beat workflow is shown free in official tutorials.</p>
   435→    <div class="tuts">
   436→      <div class="tuts-title">Tutorials (verified)</div>
   437→      <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HSwOE5WXCUU">How To Sync Beat in CapCut PC &mdash; youtube.com/watch?v=HSwOE5WXCUU</a>
   438→      <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZCYBTcyOGAA">CapCut Beat Sync Tutorial (manual + auto) &mdash; youtube.com/watch?v=ZCYBTcyOGAA</a>
   439→    </div>
   440→  </div>
   441→  <div class="card">
   442→    <div class="card-title">Route B &mdash; DaVinci Resolve free + beat plugin <span class="pill">Markers</span></div>
   443→    <div class="fact-row">
   444→      <span class="fact">Free</span><span class="fact">Difficulty 3 / 5</span><span class="fact">Windows only</span>
   445→    </div>
   446→    <p>The community plugin <b>davinci-beat-marker-plugin</b>
   447→    (github.com/urmamge/davinci-beat-marker-plugin) detects beats and bars in any song on
   448→    your Resolve timeline and places color-coded markers automatically. A one-click
   449→    PowerShell installer sets up everything (Python plus the librosa detection engine) &mdash;
   450→    no coding. The README confirms it works in the free version of Resolve, but note:
   451→    Blackmagic tightened free-version scripting from Resolve 19.1 onward, so test on the
   452→    version you run before relying on it in production.</p>
   453→    <div class="tuts">
   454→      <div class="tuts-title">Tutorial (verified)</div>
   455→      <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pcWbYHVadWE">FREE Automatic Beat Markers in DaVinci Resolve &mdash; youtube.com/watch?v=pcWbYHVadWE</a>
   456→    </div>
   457→  </div>
   458→  <div class="card">
   459→    <div class="card-title">Route C &mdash; a plain timestamp list <span class="pill">Any editor</span></div>
   460→    <div class="fact-row">
   461→      <span class="fact">Free</span><span class="fact">Difficulty 2 / 5</span><span class="fact">TXT output</span>
   462→    </div>
   463→    <p><b>Audacity</b> (audacityteam.org): open the song, <b>Analyze &rarr; Beat Finder</b>, then
   464→    <b>File &rarr; Export &rarr; Export Labels</b> for a tab-separated .txt of timestamps. Audacity&rsquo;s
   465→    manual honestly calls Beat Finder &ldquo;rough and ready&rdquo; on modern compressed music &mdash; treat
   466→    it as onset hints, not gospel. For true beat timestamps, the command-line tool <b>aubio</b>
   467→    ships a Windows build (aubio.org/bin/0.4.6/aubio-0.4.6-win64.zip) whose <b>aubiotrack</b>
   468→    command prints one beat time per line &mdash; accurate, but typing commands in cmd.exe earns
   469→    it a 4/5 difficulty.</p>
   470→  </div>
   471→  <p class="small-note">
   472→    <b>Paid reference:</b> BeatEdit for Premiere Pro is USD 99.99 and BeatEdit for DaVinci Resolve
   473→    is USD 49.99 (aescripts.com) &mdash; both excellent, but the Resolve edition now requires Studio 19.1+,
   474→    so the free plugin above is the sensible starting point. Skip them until editing revenue pays for them.
   475→  </p>

   477→  <!-- ---------- 5. CHAPTERS + PIPELINE ---------- -->
   478→  <div class="chapter-header">
   479→    <div class="section-tag">Part 05</div>
   480→    <div class="section-title">YouTube Chapters + The Full Pipeline</div>
   481→  </div>
   482→  <p class="body-text">
   483→    Timestamps are not only for your editing timeline &mdash; YouTube turns them into clickable
   484→    chapters on the watch page, and viewers love them on music videos. The official rules
   485→    (support.google.com/youtube/answer/9884579) are simple: the first timestamp must be
   486→    <b>00:00</b>, you need at least <b>three</b> in ascending order, each chapter must be at
   487→    least <b>ten seconds</b> long, and the list goes straight into the video description.
   488→    Your structure map from Part 02 is the chapters list &mdash; write it once, use it twice.
   489→  </p>
   490→  <div class="card">
   491→    <div class="card-title">The full JJP pipeline for one YuE2 song <span class="pill">Six steps</span></div>
   492→    <div class="pipe-step"><div class="pipe-num">1</div><div class="pipe-body"><b>Generate on Kaggle.</b> Run the YuE2 ComfyUI canvas, queue the prompt, and download the complete run ZIP from the wrapper &mdash; the WAV lives inside.</div></div>
   493→    <div class="pipe-step"><div class="pipe-num">2</div><div class="pipe-body"><b>Map the structure.</b> Upload the WAV to Moises Song Parts (or SONOTELLER once the song is on YouTube). Write down intro / verse / chorus / outro times.</div></div>
   494→    <div class="pipe-step"><div class="pipe-num">3</div><div class="pipe-body"><b>Tap the lyrics.</b> QuickLRC tap-along with your written lyrics; export SRT and LRC.</div></div>
   495→    <div class="pipe-step"><div class="pipe-num">4</div><div class="pipe-body"><b>Edit on the beat.</b> CapCut Desktop: Beat Sync for markers, import the SRT, and land your hook visual exactly where the structure map says the chorus starts.</div></div>
   496→    <div class="pipe-step"><div class="pipe-num">5</div><div class="pipe-body"><b>Export and publish.</b> Render, upload to YouTube, keep the song unlisted first if you want a SONOTELLER cross-check.</div></div>
   497→    <div class="pipe-step"><div class="pipe-num">6</div><div class="pipe-body"><b>Chapters in the description.</b> Start at 00:00, three-plus timestamps, ten-second minimum &mdash; straight from step 2&rsquo;s notes.</div></div>
   498→  </div>
